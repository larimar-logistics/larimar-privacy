Security Incident Response Policy

Larimar Decorator · Version 1.0 · Effective September 21, 2026

This policy defines how Larimar Logistics (“Larimar”) detects, responds to, and learns from security incidents affecting the Larimar Decorator app and the data it processes. Its goals are to limit harm, meet Larimar’s legal and contractual notification obligations, and restore normal operation quickly.

Scope

This policy covers any event that affects the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of:

  • Merchant and shopper personal data processed by the App (order data, customer contact and shipping data, shopper design data);
  • the App’s code, infrastructure, databases, file storage, secrets, and integrations;
  • staff accounts and credentials with access to the above.

It applies to all Larimar personnel and contractors with access to these systems.

Definitions

  • Security event — an observable occurrence that may have security relevance (an alert, an unexpected error, a failed-login spike). Most events are not incidents.
  • Security incident — an event confirmed, or reasonably suspected, to compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of in-scope systems or data.
  • Personal data breach — a security incident leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data.

Roles and responsibilities

  • Incident Response Lead (IRL) — the engineering owner responsible for the Larimar platform. Owns the response, classifies severity, coordinates responders, and decides on containment and notification. A designated backup covers when the primary is unavailable.
  • Responders — engineers who investigate, contain, and remediate under the IRL’s direction.
  • Communications owner — drafts and sends notifications to merchants, Shopify, regulators, and affected individuals as directed by the IRL (may be the IRL on a small team).
  • Executive sponsor — a member of Larimar leadership who approves external notifications and decisions with legal or financial exposure.

Any staff member who discovers a possible incident is responsible for reporting it immediately.

Severity classification

Severity Definition Examples
SEV-1 (Critical) Confirmed or likely personal data breach, or a full outage / active compromise Unauthorized access to the production database; a leaked production secret; confirmed data exfiltration
SEV-2 (High) Serious risk contained, or partial service impact, without confirmed data exposure Exploitable vulnerability in production; suspicious access under investigation; a data-scoping defect
SEV-3 (Low) Limited-impact or suspected event with no evidence of data exposure Isolated malicious traffic blocked; low-risk dependency vulnerability; anomalous but explained activity

When in doubt, classify up and de-escalate later.

Detection and reporting

Incidents may be detected through system logs and monitoring, error alerts, Shopify or merchant reports, a sub-processor’s notice, or a report from a researcher or member of the public. Any suspected incident is reported immediately to christopher.rodriguez@larimarlogistics.com and to the Incident Response Lead. The IRL acknowledges the report and opens an incident record without undue delay.

Response process

For every incident the IRL runs these phases and records each step, with timestamps:

  1. Triage & classify — confirm it is an incident, assign a severity, assemble responders.
  2. Contain — stop the spread: revoke or rotate compromised credentials and secrets, disable affected access, isolate affected components, or block malicious traffic; preserve evidence where feasible.
  3. Eradicate — remove the root cause: patch the vulnerability, remove unauthorized access, fix the defect.
  4. Recover — restore systems and data from known-good state, verify integrity, confirm normal operation and monitoring.
  5. Assess data impact — determine whether personal data was affected, whose, what categories, and how many records.
  6. Notify — execute the notifications required for the confirmed impact and severity.
  7. Close & review — confirm resolution and hold a post-incident review.

Notification obligations

  • Merchants. Where a personal data breach affects data Larimar processes on a merchant’s behalf, Larimar notifies the affected merchant(s) without undue delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware, per the Data Processing Addendum, describing the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate numbers affected, the likely consequences, the measures taken, and a contact point. Information may be provided in phases as it becomes known.
  • Shopify. Larimar notifies Shopify of a security incident affecting the App or merchant data in line with Shopify’s Partner Program and API terms, within the timeframe Shopify requires.
  • Regulators. Where Larimar acts as a controller and a breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals, Larimar notifies the competent supervisory authority as required by law. Where Larimar acts as a processor, it supports the merchant’s notification.
  • Affected individuals. Where required by law, affected individuals are notified — by Larimar where it is the controller, or in support of the merchant where the merchant is the controller.

External notifications are approved by the executive sponsor and, where warranted, reviewed by legal counsel before sending.

Evidence and records

For each incident Larimar keeps a record of the timeline, severity, systems and data involved, actions taken, decisions and their rationale, notifications sent, and the outcome. Relevant logs and evidence are preserved for as long as needed for investigation, legal, and regulatory purposes. Records are kept confidential and access is limited.

Post-incident review

Within a reasonable period after closing a SEV-1 or SEV-2 incident (target: 10 business days), responders hold a blameless review that identifies the root cause, what worked and what did not, and concrete follow-up actions with owners and due dates. Follow-up actions are tracked to completion and feed back into Larimar’s security measures and this policy.

Testing and maintenance

This policy is reviewed at least annually and after any significant incident. Larimar periodically exercises the process — for example through a tabletop walkthrough of a realistic scenario — to confirm that roles, contacts, and steps still work, and updates the policy accordingly.
